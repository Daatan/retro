Who saw $120 oil and €200 gas

Six months before the invasion: 264 rated articles that quote banks, agencies, companies and the press on oil and European gas prices. Each was read in full and rated separately on two questions: would Brent settle at or above $120, and would TTF settle above EUR 200/MWh in 2022. Both thresholds were crossed on 7 March 2022.

How this was computed

Search

A list of 146 domains: business and energy press, agencies, banks, think tanks, and Russian-language and European outlets. GDELT GKG via BigQuery returned 12 432 distinct URLs with energy headlines. Bloomberg, the Financial Times and the Wall Street Journal were barely represented in GDELT for these topics (6 rows, 1 usable article).

Filter

Keywords and Claude Haiku on headlines: 2 551 candidates were narrowed to 817. Full texts were downloaded; 496 got a rating. We dropped irrelevant items, articles that only describe prices, and articles with traces of edits after 24 February. Dates were checked against the page itself for every year, not only 2021-2022.

Rating

The model reads the text as of the publication date and returns the main voice's position from -1 to +1 separately for each question. When a price is named, a fixed rule applies: for example a Brent forecast below $90 gives -0.6, $105-119 gives -0.1, $130 and above gives +0.6; the article's tone shifts it by at most 0.2. If the speaker stated a probability, that is used. For a range, the highest price the speaker treats as likely is used.

P = 0.5 + 0.5 × position

Whose voice

P belongs to the article's main voice, not to the outlet or the author: a TASS item on Gazprom's words gets Gazprom's P. P of 0.6 or more is "expects above the threshold", P of 0.4 or less is "does not expect". The aggregates on this page use the full pool of 264 rated rows, published as pool.csv (date, group, region, P for Brent and TTF).

What is missing

  • Bloomberg, the Financial Times and the Wall Street Journal are barely represented: those sites barely appeared in GDELT for the topic, and what exists in Wayback is mostly paywalled.
  • Texts of 166 table articles come from live pages and 45 from the Wayback archive before 25 February. Live pages may have been edited, so articles with traces of later edits were dropped.
  • The opening (first 120 characters) of 2 of 211 quotes was not found in the text; they are marked in the table. A further 57 quotes are excerpts from two places in an article stitched with "..."; 113 quotes in all are not fully verbatim.
  • Most articles name neither a price nor a probability. For them P is the model's reading: "the winter will be very hard" gives clearly above 0.5, but does not prove the author had EUR 200 in mind. P should not be read as a calibrated probability.
  • Ratings were set by a model. It sometimes mixes up the market (US gas instead of European) or takes a conditional scenario for a forecast. We removed by hand 12 articles whose TTF rating rested on US gas and 4 that held no forecast; other cases may remain. An independent check of 44 random rows against their texts found a defect in about half (95% CI 34-62%), a quarter of them changing what the dot says.
  • The price bands in the method are guidance for the model, not a mechanical formula: for a share of priced ratings P sits further from the band than the tone allowance permits (in the audit: 36% of priced Brent legs, before the clean-up). In the audit, recomputing P strictly by the rule lowered the Brent "expect above" share from about 28% to 17% and the TTF share from 62% to 54% (the TTF figure is from before the US-gas clean-up). Read the shares as direction, not as calibrated probabilities.
  • Brent here is the front-month future (Yahoo Finance BZ=F). Dated Brent spot on the same day, 8 March, was above $130, so a $130 threshold was not unambiguous; we used $120.
